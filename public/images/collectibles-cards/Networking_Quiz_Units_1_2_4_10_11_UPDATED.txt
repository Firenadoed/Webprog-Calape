=========================
UNIT 1: INTRODUCTION TO DATA COMMUNICATIONS AND NETWORKING
=========================

Q1: Raw facts collected that become meaningful only after processing.
A1: Data

Q2: Processed data that enables decision-making.
A2: Information

Q3: What is the main objective of data communication and networking?
A3: To enable seamless exchange of data between any two points in the world.

Q4: What are the four fundamental characteristics of effective data communication?
A4: Delivery, Accuracy, Timeliness, and Jitter

Q5: List the five components of a data communication system.
A5: Message, Sender, Receiver, Transmission Medium, and Protocol

Q6: Rules that define what, how, and when data is communicated.
A6: Protocol

Q7: Mention the three types of data flow directions.
A7: Simplex, Half Duplex, and Full Duplex

Q8: A device that sends data (message) in a communication system.
A8: Sender

Q9: What are the categories of computer networks based on size?
A9: LAN (Local Area Network), MAN (Metropolitan Area Network), and WAN (Wide Area Network)

Q10: Define LAN.
A10: A network covering a small geographic area, such as an office or building.

Q11: Define WAN.
A11: A network covering a large geographic area, such as a country or continent.

Q12: Define MAN.
A12: A network larger than a LAN but smaller than a WAN, usually spanning a city.

Q13: What are the three key elements of a protocol?
A13: Syntax, Semantics, and Timing

Q14: What are the two categories of standards in networking?
A14: De facto (by convention) and De jure (by law)

Q15: Name any three standard organizations in networking.
A15: ISO, IEEE, and ITU-T

UNIT 2: SIGNALS
-------------------------

Q1: What are the two types of data?
A1: Analog and Digital

Q2: What are the two types of signals?
A2: Analog Signal and Digital Signal

Q3: Signals that repeat themselves after a fixed time period.
A3: Periodic Signals

Q4: Signals that do not repeat after a fixed time.
A4: Non-periodic Signals

Q5: Characteristics of an analog signal.
A5: Amplitude, Frequency, and Phase

Q6: What is the relationship between frequency and period?
A6: They are inversely proportional (f = 1/T).

Q7: Define wavelength.
A7: The distance a signal travels in one complete cycle.

Q8: What is a composite signal?
A8: A combination of two or more simple sine waves with different frequencies, amplitudes, and phases.

Q9: Define bit rate.
A9: The number of bits transmitted per second.

Q10: Define baud rate.
A10: The number of signal changes per second.

Q11: When is bit rate equal to baud rate?
A11: When each signal change represents one bit.

Q12: What is baseband transmission?
A12: Transmission of digital data directly over a lowpass channel without modulation.

Q13: What is broadband transmission?
A13: Converting digital signals to analog before transmission through a bandpass channel.

Q14: What is attenuation?
A14: The reduction in signal strength over distance.

Q15: Define distortion.
A15: The change in shape of a signal during transmission.

UNIT 4: NETWORK MODELS (OSI MODEL)
-------------------------

Q1: What does OSI stand for?
A1: Open Systems Interconnection

Q2: How many layers does the OSI model have?
A2: Seven

Q3: List the seven layers of the OSI model (top to bottom).
A3: Application, Presentation, Session, Transport, Network, Data Link, Physical

Q4: Which layer is responsible for end-to-end process delivery?
A4: Transport Layer

Q5: Which layer handles logical addressing and routing?
A5: Network Layer

Q6: Which layer is responsible for hop-to-hop delivery?
A6: Data Link Layer

Q7: Which layer provides services like translation, encryption, and compression?
A7: Presentation Layer

Q8: Which layer establishes, manages, and terminates sessions?
A8: Session Layer

Q9: Which layer provides user access to network services?
A9: Application Layer

Q10: Which layer deals with physical connection, bit transmission, and electrical specifications?
A10: Physical Layer

Q11: What is encapsulation in networking?
A11: The process of adding headers (and sometimes trailers) to data as it passes through layers.

Q12: What is peer-to-peer communication in OSI?
A12: Communication between corresponding layers of sender and receiver.

Q13: What is the main responsibility of the network layer?
A13: Source-to-destination delivery of packets.

Q14: What does the data link layer divide data into?
A14: Frames

Q15: What does the transport layer divide data into?
A15: Segments

UNIT 10: TRANSMISSION MEDIA
-------------------------

Q1: Define transmission media.
A1: The physical path through which data travels from sender to receiver.

Q2: What are the two broad categories of transmission media?
A2: Guided (wired) and Unguided (wireless)

Q3: Give examples of guided media.
A3: Twisted pair cable, Coaxial cable, Optical fiber

Q4: Give examples of unguided media.
A4: Radio waves, Microwaves, Infrared

Q5: What are the two types of twisted pair cables?
A5: Unshielded Twisted Pair (UTP) and Shielded Twisted Pair (STP)

Q6: Which guided medium offers the highest transmission speed and bandwidth?
A6: Optical Fiber Cable

Q7: Mention one advantage of optical fiber.
A7: High bandwidth, immune to electromagnetic interference

Q8: What are the three frequency ranges of wireless communication?
A8: Radio, Microwave, and Infrared

Q9: Which unguided medium is used in satellite communication?
A9: Microwaves

Q10: What is the disadvantage of optical fiber cables?
A10: Expensive installation and maintenance

UNIT 11: NETWORK TOPOLOGIES
-------------------------

Q1: Define network topology.
A1: The physical or logical arrangement of nodes and connections in a network.

Q2: What are the two types of network topology arrangements?
A2: Physical topology and Logical topology

Q3: In which topology are all devices connected to a single central cable?
A3: Bus topology

Q4: In which topology are all devices connected to a central device such as a hub or switch?
A4: Star topology

Q5: Topology where each device is connected to exactly two others forming a circular path.
A5: Ring topology

Q6: Topology that connects every device to every other device.
A6: Mesh topology

Q7: Topology that combines two or more different types of topologies.
A7: Hybrid topology

Q8: Topology where all nodes share the same transmission medium.
A8: Bus topology

Q9: Which topology is most fault-tolerant?
A9: Mesh topology

Q10: Which topology is most commonly used in modern LANs?
A10: Star topology

Q11: What happens if the central hub fails in a star topology?
A11: The entire network stops functioning.

Q12: Mention one disadvantage of ring topology.
A12: Failure of a single node can disrupt the entire network.

Q13: What is the main advantage of bus topology?
A13: Easy to install and requires less cable.

Q14: What is the main disadvantage of mesh topology?
A14: Expensive due to high cabling cost and complexity.

Q15: What topology is suitable for large organizations needing reliability and scalability?
A15: Hybrid topology
